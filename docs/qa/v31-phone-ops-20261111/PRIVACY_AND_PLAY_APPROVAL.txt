Sharing rationale and limitation
This is a documented compliance recommendation, not a claim of Google approval.
Google excludes transfers to processors acting only on the developer's behalf.
Twilio's current DPA sections 3.3–3.4 also provide for independent-controller
processing of phone-bearing communications usage/content for service/security
and improvement purposes. Therefore do not assume a blanket processor exemption
or mark Phone "not shared" merely because it is sent by Supabase rather than a
native SDK. The conservative shared declaration covers that independent use.
The proposal does not add advertising, sale of numbers or new application analytics.

Retention: accurate now, no invented seven-day promise
The proposed notice describes published Twilio defaults (400-day access plus
possible older archives) and provider deletion-backup limits. Actual account
"Message Records Data Access and Backup" settings still need read-only checking;
browser control is unavailable in this turn. No paid data-control add-on, provider
deletion schedule or new cron has been enabled or made a prerequisite.
An optional later reduction can be considered with its exact control/cost review.
The founder-approved 90-day policy is only routine operational incidents. It
does not apply to Auth users, SMS logs, financial/security data or held records.
Neither account erasure nor OTP expiry is represented as automatic deletion of
every Twilio/carrier record. Provider record deletion requests remain a manual
privacy-support workflow, with identity checks and applicable retention exceptions.

Provider-record deletion support procedure (prepared, not executed)
1. Authenticate the requester via established account controls or proportionate
   recovery checks; never accept possession of a recycled phone as sole proof.
2. Use an authorised provider operator to identify the requester's messages in
   Twilio Console; minimise exposure of numbers and message bodies. Do not export
   them into Git, public QA screenshots or general incident logs.
3. Check security, legal and financial preservation needs; explain exclusions.
4. Submit the exact permitted deletion through Twilio, recording only opaque
   request/status/date evidence; no routine broad deletion or new schedule.
5. Explain Twilio backup/legal exceptions and separate carrier records. Verify
   provider outcome before marking that portion of the privacy request complete.
This workflow is separate from the existing application Auth/profile erasure job.
