Accounting conclusions from source review and primary guidance
1. Preserve both customer prices. Do not add 10% to A$49 or A$99 or reduce the
   promised credit allocations. The verifier requires the exact approved gross
   AUD amount; an unexpected gross amount goes to REVIEW, not credit fulfilment.
2. Google's Australia guidance distinguishes Australian developers from foreign
   developers. Docked's Australian entity retains responsibility for GST on its
   Australian taxable sales. Accepted registration is not evidence that Google
   remits Docked's sales GST. Nor does a Google API tax field prove BAS liability.
3. For a supply taxable at purchase at 10% GST, the inclusive calculation is
   gross / 11: A$49 = A$44.55 plus A$4.45 GST (rounded per transaction);
   A$99 = A$90 plus A$9 GST. These are conditional accounting examples, not
   invented provider tax amounts or final advice on credit-voucher classification.
4. Existing code uses integer AUD cents and credits in separate balanced journals.
   Provider order gross, optional tax and cumulative refund amounts are retained.
   Missing provider tax stays null, not assumed zero and not filled with gross/11.
   Credits granted remain 4,900/9,900 regardless of tax or Google fee deduction.
   Pending/cancelled orders cannot create a verified paid credit entitlement;
   partial-refund uncertainty is held and full refunds are not double-counted.
5. The money journal deliberately books gross Google receivable against
   unallocated digital-value liability. Its report explicitly leaves recognised
   revenue/fees unknown and payout reconciliation false. It is not a complete
   GST/BAS or bank-settlement ledger. Nothing was relabelled as settled revenue.
6. Before sales, the accountant needs to set the treatment and attribution point
   for Docked Credits (taxable digital entitlement versus any applicable voucher
   treatment), pack redemption and the fixed-price marketplace fee. The ATO's
   voucher rules can change the tax point. Do not tax the same value twice at
   purchase and redemption; do not infer a seller's GST status from registration
   in Docked. No marketplace cash-out or new tax/business rule is authorised here.
   This is an accounting-policy decision, not another GST-registration question.
