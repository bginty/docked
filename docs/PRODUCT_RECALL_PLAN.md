## Triggers

Escalate immediately for:

- death, serious injury or serious illness associated with a supplied product;
- drowning/near drowning, electric shock, fire, thermal event, battery swelling/venting, entanglement, propeller/guard contact or loss of control/stability;
- a product that will or may injure a person in normal or reasonably foreseeable use/misuse;
- suspected failure to meet an applicable mandatory safety standard, ban, electrical/radio rule or required marking/warning;
- a supplier, regulator, carrier, laboratory or insurer safety alert/recall;
- missing/incorrect labels, instructions, guards, charger or battery;
- unapproved component/material/factory/firmware substitution;
- recurring leakage, seam/valve/handle/attachment, motor/controller, charger or battery defect; or
- evidence that a website claim/instruction could drive unsafe use.

A complaint is not dismissed because the customer did not use the word “safety” or because the product is marketed 18+.

## Immediate response

### First 0–4 hours

1. Protect people: if necessary, give the reporter clear interim “stop use / isolate” directions approved by a competent person. Tell anyone needing urgent medical help to contact emergency services on Triple Zero (000).
2. Log the report time, reporter, order, product/model/SKU, batch/serial, event, environment, injuries, product condition and evidence location. Do not speculate or admit unsupported facts.
3. Preserve the product, packaging, charger, battery, controller, manual, photos/video, correspondence and system logs. Do not alter or destructively test evidence without an agreed protocol.
4. Stop affected product/variant sales, marketing and fulfilment. Quarantine warehouse/in-transit stock and prevent automated feeds/ads from continuing supply.
5. Alert the recall lead, legal/product-safety adviser, supplier/manufacturer and insurer as required. Escalate powered/electrical/battery events to relevant specialists.
6. Start a timestamped decision/action log and restrict access to incident/customer information.

### Same day / first business day

1. Identify exact product, components and revisions; preserve a “golden” sample and evidence set.
2. Reconcile quantities manufactured/ordered, received, in stock, in transit, fulfilled, returned, replaced, disposed of and exported.
3. Identify affected and potentially affected batches/orders; where scope is uncertain, use the safer documented interim scope.
4. Assess the defect/dangerous characteristic, likely use/misuse, exposure, severity, likelihood, affected population and whether warnings/instructions are adequate.
5. Decide and document whether recall action is required; do not delay protective action while waiting for perfect data.
6. Agree which supplier will lead if multiple suppliers are affected and document each party’s duties. Docked still tracks its own customers, stock and legal obligations.
7. Draft regulator notification, consumer notice, support script, website banner, direct messages, supply-chain notice, remedy and safe-return/disposal instructions.

## Regulator and legal notification clock

The ACCC’s current official guidance says a supplier must notify the ACCC, on behalf of the Commonwealth Minister, **within 2 days after taking action to recall an unsafe consumer product**. Recall action can include stopping sale, contacting consumers or offering repair/replacement/refund. Submit the current recall notification even if some information is still being gathered, then provide updates under the regulator reference.

Separately, current guidance says a supplier must submit a mandatory report **within 2 days of becoming aware of a death, serious injury or serious illness** associated with a supplied consumer product or product-related service. Do not assume the recall notice alone always resolves every reporting route; obtain legal advice and use the current form.

If affected product was supplied overseas, current ACCC guidance also describes notice/copy obligations within 10 days. Docked’s launch market is Australia only, but verify exports/forwarded supply rather than assuming none occurred.

The two-day clock is a legal deadline, not a target for beginning preparation. Verify the calculation, weekend/public-holiday treatment, lead regulator and any electrical, state/territory, communications, dangerous-goods, workplace or other notification with counsel at the event.

## Consumer and supply-chain communication

Use wording agreed with the ACCC and advisers consistently. Communication must be early, direct, prominent and repeated if response is low.

The approved notice should:

- use the agreed “Product Safety Recall” heading and regulator-required format;
- clearly identify product name, make/model, images, distinguishing features, affected batch/serial/date codes and sale dates;
- explain the defect/dangerous characteristic and maximum potential hazard in plain language;
- state known incidents/injuries accurately;
- give an unambiguous immediate action such as stop use, switch off/isolate, do not charge, or keep away from water as approved for the issue;
- explain the simple remedy and how to obtain it without unnecessary steps;
- give safe return, collection or disposal directions, particularly for damaged/wet/swollen lithium batteries—never tell a customer to post a dangerous battery until the carrier/dangerous-goods route is approved;
- provide verified support channels and accessibility/language help; and
- link to the published ACCC notice when available.

Communication channels may include direct Shopify email/SMS to affected customers where lawfully available, a prominent top-third homepage banner and recall page, product/collection/cart messaging, transactional email, social channels actually used, paid-media retargeting where lawful, retailers/marketplaces/distributors and press or community outreach proportionate to risk. Do not expose customer identities or batch/order data publicly.

Tell every retailer, marketplace, distributor and other supply-chain party to stop supply, quarantine stock, use the approved notice/remedy, contact their customers where possible and report quantities/remedies. Alert other businesses if a faulty component may be used in their products.

## Remedy and logistics

Choose a remedy with legal advice and the ACCC. It may include repair, replacement, full refund, warning/label/instruction correction or software/firmware action, but it must remove the hazard and remain consistent with Australian Consumer Law. A recall offer does not remove a consumer’s right to another remedy available under consumer guarantees.

- Make the process simple and timely; do not impose an inappropriate inspection, return, restocking or shipping fee.
- Provide prior authorisation and safe instructions before use of the Heidelberg Heights correspondence/returns address. No walk-ins or unannounced returns.
- Separate returned product in a secure labelled quarantine area; maintain chain of custody and prevent resale.
- Use qualified repair/rework and verification; track new labels/manuals/firmware and reworked serials.
- Use approved dangerous-goods collection/packaging for batteries, chargers or electrically damaged/wet product.
- Record refund/replacement/repair, product recovery, disposal certificate and customer completion.
- Include second-hand product and products containing an affected component in scope planning.

## Tracking, reporting and adjustment

Maintain a daily/approved-cadence dashboard during the active phase:

- total affected and potentially affected units;
- stock quarantined, customer units located, remedies completed and products recovered/disposed;
- customer reach and response by channel/region;
- unresolved units and failed contacts;
- incidents, injuries, complaints and dispute outcomes;
- turnaround time and remedy inventory;
- supplier/retailer progress; and
- costs and insurer/supplier recovery.

Submit recall progress reports at the intervals the ACCC specifies and immediately advise material changes in scope, risk, remedy or communication. Current ACCC guidance requires a separate mandatory report within 2 days of awareness of a death, serious injury or serious illness; continue monitoring throughout the recall.

If response or recovery is low, broaden/repeat communication, target affected regions/channels, simplify the remedy and document the adjustment. Never close a recall solely because a planned end date arrived.

## Closure and prevention

Close only with recall-lead, legal and regulator alignment where applicable, after:

- affected stock and customer-unit reconciliation is documented;
- agreed remedy/communication targets and regulator reporting are complete;
- unresolved exposure and ongoing customer route are accepted;
- quarantined/returned goods and batteries are safely reworked, returned or disposed;
- website/feeds/listings remain corrected and no recalled stock can be reactivated;
- root cause and corrective/preventive action are verified with the supplier/manufacturer;
- testing, evidence, packaging, labels, manuals, claims and training are updated;
- insurer, financial and record-retention obligations are addressed; and
- lessons learned are incorporated into supplier onboarding, receiving inspection and SKU approval.

Any replacement or corrected product follows the full SKU approval workflow. A supplier’s claim that the issue is fixed is not release evidence.
