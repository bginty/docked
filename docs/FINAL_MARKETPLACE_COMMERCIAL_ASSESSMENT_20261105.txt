Observed implementation boundaries

* CommercialLedger.wallet() returns withdrawable:0. No commercial cash-out,
  bank transfer, general credit-to-credit gift, or third-party cash redemption
  method was found in the authenticated commercial adapter.
* Current Google products are docked_credits_4900_v1 (A$49/4,900 credits) and
  docked_credits_9900_v1 (A$99/9,900 credits). These are credit purchases; the
  internal pack prices are not A$49/A$99 direct-pack prices.
* CommercialLedger.list()/buyListing() use transfers_enabled, transaction locks,
  current ownership and hold checks. A fixed-price sale moves a card and credits
  within Docked; sellerQuote() deducts 5%, capped at 2,000, whole-credit rounding.
  There is no enabled cash redemption of seller proceeds.
* /api/marketplace calls memberMarketplace(), then memberTestSource(). The latter
  requires the private beta flags and admission. It is not an ordinary public
  commercial listing endpoint. memberCommercial currently exposes receipt,
  reconciliation and pack purchase/opening, not ledger list/buy operations.
* /api/marketplace accepts list/reprice/cancel/review-buy/buy, not trade commands.
  memberTestSource rejects offer/accept/replace-listing or non-sell preference.
  sandboxSwaps also rejects new offer/accept. Historical records are retained.
* Hosted redemptionSource cannot mutate redemption without assertLocalRedemption.
  Its hosted read forces enabled:false, available/pending:0 and no requests or
  destinations. Historical/local withdrawal examples are not public features.
* No NFT/blockchain ownership, outside-Docked card export/transfer or gift-card
  redemption service was found. Collection CSV/share screenshots do not convey
  ownership. Generic account resale or a hypothetical unauthorised black market
  is not an implemented cash-out path; abuse reports must still be investigated.
* Free Core competition entry does not purchase or issue a collectible. Rarity
  multipliers affect fantasy points. With prizes disabled, those points do not
  produce cash, physical prizes or cash-convertible reward entitlements.
* Refunds are reversals of an attributable purchase, not a user cash-out service.
  Unspent funds are removed; spent funds create an auditable review. Neither
  automatic bank charging nor unrelated card confiscation is authorised.

Google Play analysis (primary policies checked 5 November)

Google's Payments policy requires its billing system for covered digital goods,
confines purchased virtual currency to that app/game, and requires item odds
before and close to random-item purchase. Docked's pack review renders its
authoritative rarity chances and no-substitution supply-failure disclosure.
Actual native TEST purchases/acknowledgement/refunds are documented separately;
do not turn TEST success into a claim of approved commercial settlement.
https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9858738

The real-money games restriction addresses paid staking/participation for a
prize of real-world monetary value. Randomness alone is not that finding. The
staged model supplies in-game goods and free/no-prize play, not a cash contest.
Keep withdrawal, paid-entry, prize and external-redemption paths OFF. Google
review remains independent; the policy also excludes certain peer-to-peer and
auction payments from Play Billing, so do not convert an internal marketplace
into native cash payments between sellers without a separate assessment.
https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9877032?rd=1

Questionnaire guidance: paid digital goods/random items Yes; communication/UGC
Yes where supported; no enabled cash withdrawal/paid entry/cash prizes. Answer
the precise trading wording accurately: the owner observed that it includes
items traded for currency purchased with money. Do not change that to No merely
because cash-out is OFF. Do not describe this closed-ledger game as an NFT app.
The listing must state optional paid packs and which marketplace features are
actually available. No cash earnings or investment-return claims.

Australian analysis

The current Classification guidance makes paid chance-linked purchases minimum
M, while simulated gambling has a separate R18+ rule. The recorded Australian
IARC M result is consistent with paid random packs; it is not permission for
cash gambling. Docked's own adult eligibility is separate from M's advisory age.
https://www.classification.gov.au/about-us/media-and-news/news/new-classifications-for-gambling-content-video-games

Section 4 of the current Interactive Gambling Act links the relevant game
definition to money/value, chance or mixed chance/skill, and consideration.
Free/no-prize fantasy competitions do not themselves add a paid stake or a
cash/value reward. The exact secondary-market question concerns paid random
cards and their convertible value, not whether fantasy sports are named in
the product. This is an assessment of mechanics, not an assertion of exemption.
The Federal Register identifies the current compilation and future 1 January
2027 reform. The enacted 2026 reform reviewed here contains no loot-box term;
a proposed R18+ loot-box amendment is not treated as enacted law.
https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A00851/latest/text
https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2026A00072/asmade/2026-08-26/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html

ACMA's published submission says ordinary loot boxes have generally not been
regarded as IGA gambling where play is not for money/value, while acknowledging
uncertainty where items have secondary markets. This supports the staged
non-transfer configuration; it does not resolve a separately enabled market.
https://www.acma.gov.au/sites/default/files/2022-12/Sub-96-Australian-Communications-and-Media-Authority.pdf

For credits confined to purchases from Docked, a single-payee/incidental-payment
analysis is relevant. If credits settle purchases from unrelated member sellers,
do not assume that same exemption without analysing the terms and functions.
ASIC says the rights and arrangement determine financial-product treatment.
No AFSL requirement for these specific game credits is asserted by this review,
and no exemption is fabricated. This is a precise transfer-activation question.
https://www.asic.gov.au/regulatory-resources/digital-transformation/digital-assets-financial-products-and-services
https://www.aph.gov.au/DocumentStore.ashx?id=7594481e-5528-4d7f-b6eb-8a406bcd2658&subId=707301

Refund-after-spending holds must remain proportionate, reviewable and disclosed.
Consumer guarantees are not removed by opening a pack, spending credits or
terms saying no refunds. Keep support escalation and statutory remedies, and
do not make automated recovery a blanket refusal to provide a lawful remedy.
https://www.accc.gov.au/consumers/problem-with-a-product-or-service-you-bought/repair-replace-refund-cancel
