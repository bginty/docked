Common questions

Plain-English answers borrowers search before making a decision.

How much can I borrow for a home loan?

Your borrowing power usually depends on income, expenses, other debts, dependants, credit history, deposit, and lender policy. A calculator can show a rough range, but a broker needs to check lender rules before relying on the number.

Is refinancing worth it if my rate has changed?

Refinancing may help if the repayment saving beats switching costs and the new loan still suits your plans. Compare monthly savings, annual savings, loan features, fees, cashback conditions, and break-even time.

What deposit do I need for a home loan?

Many borrowers aim for a 20% deposit to reduce the chance of lenders mortgage insurance, but smaller deposits may still be possible depending on lender policy, income, loan type, and government scheme eligibility.

Fixed or variable home loan: which is better?

Fixed loans can help with repayment certainty, while variable loans may provide more flexibility. Some borrowers split the loan. The better fit depends on risk comfort, repayment plans, cashflow, and available loan features.

What documents do brokers usually ask for?

Common documents include ID, payslips, bank statements, tax returns for self-employed borrowers, loan statements, rates notices, rental income evidence, and details of assets, debts, and living expenses.

Can self-employed borrowers get a home loan?

Yes, but lenders usually review business income differently. A broker may compare full-doc and alt-doc options, business financials, BAS, tax returns, income stability, and loan purpose.
